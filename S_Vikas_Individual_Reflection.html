Business Objective

Use six months of historical loan decisions to identify clear-cut declines that may be removed from manual review, while limiting the risk of wrongly turning away creditworthy applicants. The aim is to balance safe screening with analyst time savings—not simply to maximize model accuracy.

Step 5.4 — Logistic Regression Pre-Screener

1. Target and features: The target was encoded as Declined = 1 and Approved = 0. Features included credit score, new-to-credit flag (is_ntc), FOIR, loan-to-income, delinquencies, active loans, job tenure, age, loan tenure, city tier, employment type, and loan purpose. Decision was the target, not a feature; application ID, date, city, and gender were excluded from model inputs.

2. Train-test split: An 80/20 stratified split with random_state=42 produced 9,600 training rows and 2,400 test rows. Training data fitted the model and preprocessing; the held-out test data measured performance.

3. Preprocessing: Missing income was filled using employment-type medians with an overall fallback. Missing credit scores were filled with the training median while the is_ntc flag preserved the fact that those applicants had no score. Numeric features were scaled and categorical features were one-hot encoded. A pipeline kept these transformations together with Logistic Regression.

Model results at the default 0.50 threshold:

The key risk at the default threshold is the 224 false positives: applicants historically approved by the bank but predicted as declined (17.62% of approvals). This is why accuracy alone is insufficient for a lending decision.

Step 5.5 — Auto-Decline Threshold

The model produces a probability of decline. A threshold determines how high that probability must be before an application is flagged for automatic decline. The assignment requires that no more than 5% of the auto-declined group were historically approved.

Threshold Auto-declined Wrongly declined
0.50 41.38% 18.73%
0.60 35.33% 15.21%
0.70 29.33% 10.80%
0.80 22.62% 5.89%
0.85 (Selected) 19.04% 4.81%
0.90 14.71% 3.12%
0.95 9.92% 1.68%

Threshold 0.85 is selected because it is the optimal threshold that satisfies the 5% risk rule (4.81% wrong-decline rate) while auto-declining the maximum volume of rejections.

Monthly business impact:

  • Applications screened: 3,000 × 19.04% ≈ 571 applications per month.
  • Analyst hours saved: 571 × 25 minutes ÷ 60 ≈ 238 hours per month (~1.5 full-time underwriters).
  • Observed wrong-decline rate: 4.81% (strictly within the bank's 5.0% risk constraint).
  • New-to-credit safeguard: NTC accuracy is only 56.7% (vs 81.2% for scored borrowers); therefore, NTC applicants must be routed to human review.

Steps 5.6 & 5.7 — Fairness Check and Credit Policy Briefing

Fairness Analysis: Compared approval patterns across demographic groups. Historical approval was 54.1% for males vs. 50.4% for females. This 3.7 percentage-point gap stems from structural differences: median female income was ₹45,000 (vs. ₹55,000 male), and gig employment share was 21.4% (vs. 11.6% male). At the selected threshold, model auto-decline rates were near parity (~15.0% male vs. 15.6% female). Gender was strictly excluded from features.

Credit Policy Briefing Guidance:

  • Threshold: Auto-decline only at high confidence (0.85+): frees ~571 applications and ~238 analyst hours monthly at 4.81% error.
  • Top drivers: FOIR ceiling enforced at 50%; credit score floor applied below 650.
  • Keep with humans: Mandatory human underwriting for New-to-Credit applicants.
  • Before go-live: Execute 30-day silent pilot, monthly demographic fairness tracking, and model validation on fresh out-of-time data.
